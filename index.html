What we install

Three ways to light a car

Everything else on a build is a combination of these three.

Starlight headliner

Real fiber optic, pulled through the headliner one strand at a time and trimmed flush with the fabric. Sized so the back of the roof reads as full as the front. Twinkle, shooting stars, and any color you want — including a logo or a name spelled out in the roof.

  • Twinkle
  • Shooting stars
  • Custom shapes
  • Phone control

Ambient lighting

Dash, doors, pillars and footwells — one continuous line, not a row of dots. It sits down in the trim gap, so you see the light and never the strip. One color across every zone, a slow fade, or the whole spectrum end to end.

  • Dash
  • Doors
  • Footwells
  • Pillars

Underglow

Rockers, wheel wells and grille, sealed to survive a Florida summer and everything the road throws at it. Runs a flat color, a slow fade, or the full spectrum wheel to wheel — off the same phone as everything else.

  • Wheel wells

Process

How an install runs

Four steps from the first message to driving it out lit.

  1. Step 01

    Send the car and a photo of the roof

    Year, make, model, and one picture looking straight up. Sunroof, panoramic or solid changes the entire layout, so that photo does most of the work. You get a real number back, not a range.

  2. Step 02

    Pick a day

    Tell us when the car can be without you and we hold that date. You'll know before you book whether yours is a one-day job or two.

  3. Step 03

    Headliner out, fiber in

    The headliner and pillar trim come out in one piece. Every strand is pulled through from the back, glued off and routed to the engine, which gets mounted somewhere dry and reachable — then it all goes back on factory clips.

  4. Step 04

    Pick it up lit

    Before you leave we set the scenes together on your phone: the daily color, the brightness you want at night, and the one you turn on for photos.

FAQ

Before you book

Everything people ask before they hand the car over.

Does taking the headliner out wreck it?

No — it's built to come out. The board unclips, the fiber gets pulled through from the back where nobody sees it, and each strand is trimmed flush with the fabric. Once it's back in, you can't tell it ever left the car until you turn the lights on.

Will it work on my car?

Almost certainly. What decides the job is the roof, not the badge — solid, sunroof, panoramic and full glass all lay out differently, and that's what changes the point count and the routing. Send the year, make and model with a photo looking straight up and we'll tell you exactly what yours takes before you commit to anything.

How many stars do I need?

It's set by the roof, not by a menu. A sedan headliner and a truck cab need very different counts to look equally full, and a sunroof cutout changes where they can go at all. Send the photo and we'll give you the number for your car.

Can you see them during the day?

Yes — quieter than at night, but they're there. There's a clip in the work section above — Star roof — filmed at noon with the garage door open and nothing done to it.

Is underglow legal in Florida?

Mostly, with limits. Florida restricts lights showing red or blue toward the front of the car, and anything flashing or rotating — that’s reserved for emergency vehicles. So the build gets a street scene that stays inside that, and your reds and blues live on a separate scene for shows and meets. Worth confirming for your own setup before you drive it.

Will it drain my battery?

Not when it's wired right. Power gets tapped to a switched source, so the whole system dies with the ignition instead of sitting live off an always-on circuit overnight.

What if a point goes out later?

Bring it back. Fiber doesn't burn out the way an LED does — a dead point is nearly always the engine or a connection at the engine end, and both are quick to sort.
