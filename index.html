01 — Install

Three things, done properly

Everything else on a build is a combination of these.

Photo 01

Starlight headliner

Real fiber optic, pulled through the headliner one strand at a time and trimmed flush with the fabric. Sized and spread so the back of the roof reads as full as the front instead of thinning out over the rear seats.

Every roof gets lit on the stand and checked before it goes back in the car.

Photo 02

Ambient lighting

Dash, doors, pillars and footwells — one continuous line, not a row of dots. It sits down in the trim gap, so you see the light and never the strip that makes it.

One color across every zone, a slow fade, or the full spectrum end to end. Whatever you set it to is what it comes back to next time you start the car.

  • Dash
  • Doors
  • Footwells
  • Pillars
Photo 03

Suede retrim

Headliner, pillars and visors rewrapped in black suede. It's the difference between stars on a gray ceiling and stars on a night sky — the backdrop goes dead black and the points read twice as bright.

Worth doing on anything that left the factory in light gray. Teslas especially.

02 — Process

How it runs

Four steps from the first message to driving it out lit.

  1. 01

    Send the car and a photo of the roof

    Year, make, model, and one picture looking straight up. Sunroof, panoramic or solid changes the entire layout, so that photo does most of the work. You get a real number back, not a range.

  2. 02

    Pick a day

    Tell us when the car can be without you and we hold that date. You'll know before you book whether yours is a one-day job or two.

  3. 03

    Headliner out, fiber in

    The headliner and pillar trim come out in one piece. Every strand is pulled through from the back, glued off and routed to the engine, which gets mounted somewhere dry and reachable — then it all goes back on factory clips.

  4. 04

    Pick it up lit

    Before you leave we set the scenes together on your phone: the daily color, the brightness you want at night, and the one you turn on for photos.

04 — Questions

Before you book

Everything people ask before they hand the car over.

Does taking the headliner out wreck it?

No — it's built to come out. The board unclips, the fiber gets pulled through from the back where nobody sees it, and each strand is trimmed flush with the fabric. Once it's back in, you can't tell it ever left the car until you turn the lights on.

Can you do it on a Tesla?

Teslas are most of what we do. Every model lays out differently around the glass, though, so send the model and a photo looking up and we'll tell you exactly what that roof takes before you commit to anything.

How many stars do I need?

It's set by the roof, not by a menu. A sedan headliner and a truck cab need very different counts to look equally full, and a sunroof cutout changes where they can go at all. Send the photo and we'll give you the number for your car.

Can you see them during the day?

Yes — quieter than at night, but they're there. There's a daylight clip in the work section above, filmed with the garage door open and nothing done to it.

Will it drain my battery?

Not when it's wired right. Power gets tapped to a switched source, so the whole system dies with the ignition instead of sitting live off an always-on circuit overnight.

What if a point goes out later?

Bring it back. Fiber doesn't burn out the way an LED does — a dead point is nearly always the engine or a connection at the engine end, and both are quick to sort.
